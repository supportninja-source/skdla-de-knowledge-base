## Price inquiries and callback commitments

Source timestamp 00:00:02

Pricing varies by customer and office, and the lab described more than 40 price lists at the time. Ordinary CSRs did not necessarily have access to view all pricing in ABS. Gather the practice, account relationship, whether it is new or existing, and the specific question, then escalate for an authorized callback instead of inventing a price.

A DSO relationship does not prove every office under it is onboarded. Ask whether the specific location has used the lab before and which DSO it belongs to. Distinguish the parent relationship from the individual location.

Avoid a definitive callback promise that may be missed. The recommended language is to call as soon as more is known. Still own the follow-up. A patient in the chair with a late case takes precedence over a routine proactive inquiry. Use the tracker for investigation and oversight rather than assuming every case needs a callback.

## Open ABS and prepare Case Inquiry

Source timestamp 00:12:37

Open Evo Data, not Evo Touch. Log in with the assigned individual ABS account and complete the ABS clock-in prompt. The trainer states that this clocks the user into ABS only; it is not a substitute for other attendance requirements.

Go to Cases and then Case Inquiry. Widen the date range so older and future-due cases are visible. The demonstration used a start date in the previous year and an end date at least three months ahead, then refreshed the data. Those are search-range examples rather than fixed dates to reuse forever.

Case numbers identify individual cases internally; pan IDs identify their physical trays and product-routing colors. Do not expect a calling office to know these internal numbers. An office might have an invoice number. Search external inquiries primarily from the patient and practice or doctor details.

## Understand pans and product routing

Source timestamp 00:19:14

The historical pan-color explanation maps white to zirconia-type crowns, gray to e.max, black to implant crowns, blue to metal-related work such as PFM or gold, yellow to removables and dentures, red to repair or remake, pink to diagnostic wax-up, beige and orange to removable work, and green to samples rather than live patient cases. Use the actual lab key because product names in the captions are imperfect.

The planner decides repair versus remake. The shade example explains that a light crown may be darkened by adding shade, while a crown already too dark cannot simply have shade removed and may need remaking. This is a technical distinction to recognize in records, not permission for a CSR to choose treatment.

Doctor Last may contain either a practice name or a doctor surname because of inconsistent historical entry. Use a few patient-name letters to allow for spelling errors or reversed names, then verify the shipping office. Confirm Ship To rather than Bill To when determining where the patient’s case belongs.

## Investigate the prescription production and shipping

Source timestamp 00:27:29

Case Info provides the patient, office, internal identifiers, order information, original prescription due date, scheduled delivery, and shipping details. If a caller gives a different due date, examine View Images and the original prescription before contradicting the office.

Handwritten prescriptions were described as typical of analog cases; typed scanner prescriptions typically identify digital cases. Steps and Services shows production steps, technician scans, actual dates, and scheduled dates. Compare actual progress with the schedule to judge whether the case is on track.

If it is ahead of schedule, give a confident scheduled-date update. If a step is late, do not assume the whole case will miss delivery; put it on the runner tracker for investigation and possible expedited handling. This page is the road map used for status calls.

Check Invoice even if Case Status still says Scheduled. ABS can take time to update Completed, and invoices may appear before tracking is added. The trainer suggested checking again in roughly an hour in an ordinary example, but this is not a universal tracking SLA.

The session’s shipping rule of thumb was invoicing before 3 p.m. lab time for ordinary same-day processing, with FedEx pickup around 4:30 p.m. A case invoiced after 3 p.m. without tracking might not ship that day. If it must leave for the next day’s appointment, flag it immediately on the tracker as urgent and have the lab verify dispatch.

Ask for the patient’s chair time and verify the email address even if Doctor Preferences already contains one. An early appointment can affect whether overnight service is needed and whether the office must consider a later appointment. Tracking may show FedEx, historical UPS, or a named local driver; use the daily driver list to cross-check local delivery.

## Use the four trained activity types

Source timestamp 00:51:47

Customer Service records an ordinary interaction: what the caller or email needed, what was checked, and the action taken. Keep the subject and note concise. In this recording the trainer says not to populate subtype, assignment, status, or priority for an ordinary customer-service note.

Complaint covers an experience that did not delight the customer, including a late case with no proactive communication even if the caller is calm. Use the appropriate subtype: the session names Accounting, Customer Service, Data Entry, Late, Logistics, and Technical/Quality. Gather facts before deciding the cause.

For a supposedly delivered case, inspect the carrier’s destination and compare the original case entry. If it was entered for the wrong office, that is a data-entry error rather than automatically a carrier or shipping error. Assign complaints and positive feedback to the appropriate business-unit leader.

The historic leader list distinguished full arch, crown and bridge or implants, removables, customer service, shipping, and LA cases. The recording says LA-account complaints go to its LA leader. Refer to the current business-unit directory rather than treating these historical personnel assignments as permanent.

Positive Feedback uses customer service, on-time delivery, or quality subtypes and the appropriate business-unit assignment. Technical Support captures instructions such as a missing shade or a doctor’s response and places the information on the production work ticket.

## Technical note notification remains a separate step

Source timestamp 01:01:00

Read the original technical outreach carefully, gather the office’s answer, and save it as Technical Support. That updates the ticket content, but the trainer explicitly says it does not notify the technical person. A work-email handoff to the responsible person is therefore needed.

The team discusses how remote CSRs will identify the correct technical advisor and whether the runner needs oversight. They also test assigning a technical note to see whether it causes an email notification, but no successful result is confirmed in this session. Do not substitute that unverified experiment for the explicit email handoff.

Emails are sent from the assigned SKDLA email account, not composed through an ABS New button. Case Inquiry is the core customer-service area; the subsequent onboarding segment uses other ABS maintenance and AR screens for a different role.

## Private practice account creation in ABS

Source timestamp 01:09:04

Start from the onboarding information: practice or office name, doctor, phone, email, and physical address. Go to Maintenance then Doctors. Right-click and select Add New Account. Search for an existing account or matching address before creating another record. Never select Delete Account as part of onboarding.

If no existing record matches, choose Add New and fill the main identity fields. Keep Practice Name populated; a doctor’s name can be the practice name when that is the actual business identity. The trainers acknowledge historical naming inconsistency and say future standards should remain aligned with Lola.

Record Onboarded, your initials, and the onboarding date in the demonstrated preferences area for traceability. Save before continuing and after changes. For the OC example, use the OC prefix with the numeric account component and remove trailing account letters as shown. LA onboarding is a different process and should not be forced into the OC example.

Enter and save the address before adding the phone. The California example produces a tax confirmation that the trainer accepts. Enter and save the phone, then open Email Communication Profile, right-click Add Email, enter the verified address, and save.

For the private-practice example, select email invoice, email statement, and the credit-card/ACH charge-information email option as recapped in the session, then save. DSO email options differ. The exact label of the third option is unclear in the captions and needs the visual SOP.

## Custom fields locations and AR pricing

Source timestamp 01:16:07

In Custom Two, use the scrollbar rather than the mouse wheel over fields: the trainer warns that wheel scrolling can unintentionally change selections. Set Lab ID to OC, Origin Lab to New Customer, Market Segment to Private Practice, and Strategic Key Account to the demonstrated New Customer 2026 grouping.

The strategic account executive was left blank for a private practice in this example. Under Locations, deselect the non-OC locations individually and save after changes so only OC remains for the OC account. Open OC, choose FedEx Second Day as the default carrier, and use the currently assigned account manager.

Parker was used as the temporary private-practice manager example, but the team explicitly says account ownership was changing and a current list was needed. Do not assume that name is the correct owner for every new account.

Copy the new ABS account number, then go to AR and Doctor AR Setup. Paste the account number to retrieve its setup; the instructor corrects an earlier suggestion to right-click here. Open Pricing and attach the authorized price list. OC Signature is the private-practice example, while DSOs have different lists.

Use the service-selection ellipsis to add FedEx Second Day as the auto service and save. Confirm the list from onboarding instructions rather than selecting by guesswork. Completing ABS is only the first part; the new customer still needs HubSpot and a doctor-portal login.

## Create the HubSpot contact

Source timestamp 01:29:15

In HubSpot, open Contacts, add a contact, and create a new record using the same email used in ABS. Populate the name and phone, choose the lab code OC, and enter the exact OC account number. Review the contact then create it.

The trainer uses Allen as Contact Owner because that was the instruction previously received, but the team questions the reasoning and says the process needs refinement. The intended integration is HubSpot to portal and portal to ABS, but backend issues are acknowledged. Do not assume a contact exists correctly in all systems solely because it was created in one.

## Create and verify the doctor portal account

Source timestamp 01:32:58

Return to ABS Maintenance and open Doctor Portal Login. Search with the account number and use the demonstrated account-number entry and Tab confirmation to create the portal record. Keep identity and email consistent with the ABS and HubSpot records.

The private-practice example enables balance, invoice, case, payment processing, case images, and statement access. DSO permissions differ. Generate the portal credentials through the approved account process and save. The trainer declines the immediate-send prompt so the account can be verified before the credentials are sent.

The session describes synchronization as often taking about four hours and sometimes up to 24 hours. Send credentials the same day when the account is working, or the following day if synchronization has not completed. A successful account check is required before telling the office it can log in.

The transcript includes a historical shared password and password-reset demonstration. Credential values are intentionally excluded from these notes. Follow the current authorized IT procedure for verification, password generation, resets, and customer access; do not rely on a spoken historic default.

The demonstration encounters invalid-password errors, repeated regeneration, and possible integration delay. It does not establish a clean, reliable reset sequence. The trainer mentions using an authorized admin reset function, refreshing, and retrying after synchronization. Escalate continuing problems through the supported IT workflow rather than treating repeated password attempts as a complete SOP.

## Send the verified portal credentials and distinguish DSO work

Source timestamp 01:48:50

Once the account works, open the HubSpot contact’s Email action, choose Templates, and select SKDLA Customer Portal Credentials. The template supplies the customer’s username and credentials for the authorized onboarding email. Review the correct recipient and account before sending.

The template was temporarily unavailable on one screen during backend modifications and then demonstrated on another. A missing template is a system or access issue to investigate, not a reason to send invented login information.

The trainers explicitly close by saying this is private-practice onboarding only. Strategic and DSO accounts have substantial variations requiring Lola’s account-specific recordings or supervised training. Training plans include reverse-shadowing and checking learner notes against the original written SOP. Do not infer DSO configuration from the private-practice defaults.
