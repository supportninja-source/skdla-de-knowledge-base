## Focus ownership consistency urgency and standards

Source timestamp 00:23:47

The FOCUS framework means focus on work that matters, own the outcome, use consistent methods, act urgently, and meet the service standard. A handoff still requires accountability.

A patient already in the chair is described as a 911 escalation. Escalate immediately through the available lead channel; do not rely on an ordinary tracker entry alone for an urgent chair-time situation.

## Common calls and technical follow-ups

Source timestamp 00:45:33

Common calls concern cases at risk of being late, cases already late, and offices checking whether an appointment can be met. Capture the expected due date and the actual patient appointment so production can assess urgency.

A runner connects customer service to production by locating and flagging cases and relaying technical challenges. Proactive communication to the office is needed when those challenges affect timing.

When a doctor returns a technical call, read the case notes to identify the missing prescription information or question. If the notes clearly establish what is needed, handle the follow-up rather than unnecessarily transferring the doctor.

Record the doctor's response as a technical note when it changes fabrication instructions, and email the appropriate production department or responsible person. Record that communication in the case. The technical note prints with the work ticket; an ordinary call note has a different purpose. Writing the technical note alone is not a substitute for notifying the responsible team.

## Open ABS and set the case search range

Source timestamp 00:57:54

The trainer says to log into EVO Data rather than Touch. The ABS clock-in described here identifies the user as logged into the application; it is not the payroll clock-in.

Go to Cases and then Case Inquiry. Adjust the start and end dates before searching. A default range ending today can hide future scheduled cases; a narrow historical range can hide warranty or prior-case history.

The trainer used a historical start date around the implementation of ABS in February 2024 and extended the end date several months forward. Those are search-range examples, not a rule to use an obsolete fixed date forever. Refresh after changing the dates.

## Search and verify the correct case

Source timestamp 01:10:16

Columns can be rearranged to show useful fields such as case number, PAN, doctor, and patient. Callers often do not know the ABS case number.

Search with two or three letters of a patient or doctor's name when a spelling variation might prevent an exact match. Then positively match the result to the correct doctor, office, patient, and case.

Use both the case number and PAN. PAN numbers can be duplicated across live cases, so a PAN alone is not sufficient identification. Check that the case number, PAN, patient, and doctor align.

If records appear entered under the wrong office, investigate and escalate the mismatch. The trainer models calm customer communication rather than announcing an unverified internal error. This should not be interpreted as permission to conceal a confirmed error or disclose another patient's information.

## Read production history before promising a date

Source timestamp 01:14:28

Use case information and Steps and Services to read the case's progress. The example moves through entry and review, importing the digital impression, design, nesting, model printing, and milling, with later finishing steps still required.

Compare the last production scan against the requested delivery date and appointment. An apparent pause can reflect prioritization of earlier-due work, but a scan history alone does not prove the physical case location or readiness.

Ask the runner to investigate a stalled or urgent case. If the office requests an earlier date because of an emergency, escalate that request so the runner can flag it for production. The example's remaining finishing times are illustrations, not a guaranteed turnaround for every material or case.

## Distinguish local driver delivery from carrier shipping

Source timestamp 01:18:20

Check the daily driver lists. A shipping-type field in ABS may show a carrier even when the office receives local driver delivery, so do not use that field alone to decide how the case will arrive.

For an office with a specific chair time, communicate the time to the lead or runner. Leadership may coordinate a driver route change. For a shipped case, overnight service was discussed as a possibility only if the case can be completed and dispatched in time.

An alternate delivery request is case-specific. The discussion does not establish unrestricted shipping to any address. Confirm the actual destination, completion status, available route or carrier, and feasibility through the responsible team before committing.

Use the urgent lead chat when a patient is already in the chair or an appointment is imminent. Ask whether the case is finished and invoiced and obtain an actionable update. Collaboration can help, but the trainer explicitly acknowledges that some requests cannot be met.

## Complete and monitor the runner tracker

Source timestamp 01:25:19

The demonstrated tracker includes case and PAN, representative, account information, the last scanned production step, the action needed, and the patient appointment. Explain the actual request and urgency, including any missed appointment or desired arrival time.

Red text was used in the demonstrated spreadsheet to signal escalation. The runner enters an expected date and the action taken. Check the tracker frequently before sending duplicate direct messages to someone who may already have updated it.

Keep an unresolved item visibly open in your own follow-up system. The trainer suggests marking it complete only after the follow-up has actually been handled. A calendar reminder one or two days before a committed date is an additional follow-up technique, not a substitute for ABS documentation.

The tracker shown here is a historical customer-service production tracker. It should not be confused with a data-entry assignment tracker or used to override a newer approved assignment tool.

## Keep the technical record and communications aligned

Source timestamp 01:31:32

The example compares original Rx due date, delivery date, scheduled ship date, actual production scans, case notes, and the runner tracker. A missed ship date or unexpected unscanned production step requires investigation.

When the office supplies missing information such as a shade, record it, notify the person or department holding the case, and document the notification. Ensure the responsible team receives the information needed to proceed; do not assume a note automatically releases a hold.

Review the chronological note history and tracker response so another agent can reconstruct who contacted whom, what changed, and what remains pending. An example in the captions contains inconsistent appointment dates and an arithmetic error about elapsed minutes, so those details are not treated as process rules.

## Use HubSpot with ABS and the dentist portal

Source timestamp 01:36:35

The dentist portal lets offices view cases, upload files, request changes or support, create shipping labels, and schedule pickups. Portal requests generate HubSpot tickets for assignment and tracking.

A HubSpot ticket may not contain the current production answer. Use the ticket's case information to locate the case in ABS, then read case information, hold status, Steps and Services, and call notes. In the example, a case is on hold for updated bite scans and the notes also require clarifying whether to proceed with a temporary or final restoration.

Refresh ABS when a recent case or scan is missing. The trainer recommends at least four or five refreshes during the day and specifically before concluding a newly entered case does not exist. Refreshing also helps prevent duplicate entry.

A leading zero may be needed in some case-number search fields or column formats, while others omit it. Match the actual identifier and field format; do not assume adding a zero is universally required.

At the time of this session, the portal and ABS exchanged case information, while HubSpot captured ticket activity. The trainer says to respond through the portal so the reply crosses into ABS. The relationship between systems was under development; validate the current sync and ticket-completion procedure.
