## Determine the correct logistics route

Source timestamp 00:02:08

Check the case information tab in ABS and its shipping type before offering a pickup. It may show UPS, FedEx, or local driver. A California address does not by itself mean local delivery; some offices are hours away. Use the supplied local-office list and the case history to verify eligibility.

The lab prepares a daily local-driver delivery and pickup list and shares it in the morning. The trainer corrects an initial attribution: Sylvia prepares that list. Logistics delays can sometimes cause it to arrive later. Check the actual daily list rather than assuming a regular route guarantees today’s visit.

For a local pickup request, gather the office identity, requested date, hours of operation, and the relevant case information, then post it in the local driver pickup and delivery Teams channel. Requests before 10 a.m. Pacific were described as typically achievable the same day, subject to the driver’s route. This is a conditional training guideline, not a guaranteed cutoff.

Tell the office that the pickup is being arranged and that the lab will contact them if it must occur the following day. Watch for the lab’s acknowledgment or an alternate date, and call the office back with the actual arrangement. A thumbs-up in the example represented that the driver had been contacted and the pickup arranged.

## Read the driver list and handle missing deliveries

Source timestamp 00:07:26

The daily list uses P for pickup and D for drop-off. A delivered case normally has a patient name beside it; a pickup alone may not. Both columns can be marked when the office has both incoming and outgoing cases. Certain high-volume offices have daily pickups even without calling. A stop for an outside production partner can appear without individual patient names.

Use the list to answer an office asking whether a due case is on the driver’s route. Unless a specific time is indicated, communicate end of day. Do not invent an arrival time from the order of stops. If the patient has a chair time, contact the lab so it can ask the driver whether that appointment can be accommodated.

If the case is absent from the driver list, check production completion and tracking in ABS. If it is completed, has no tracking, and belongs to a local office, ask the lab to verify or add the delivery. If it is not completed, escalate through the case inquiry chat with the appointment urgency.

The example driver sheet used pink for that day’s route and yellow for the next business day. A separate color key was promised. These colors apply to that sheet; do not confuse them with the pickup-log colors or ABS pan colors.

## Answer a case-status inquiry from evidence

Source timestamp 00:22:07

Find the case using the patient’s name plus the practice or doctor. Partial names help when spelling is uncertain. Verify the actual case and requested restoration, then inspect its due date, production progress, call notes, and tracking before answering.

Listen to the caller’s initial information and retain it so the office does not repeatedly provide the same details. Ask for spelling when needed to make sure the correct patient is selected. In the example, confirming the tooth and implant type helped distinguish a misspelled patient record.

Communicate the scheduled delivery goal with a qualification such as barring technical issues. A case close to completion still warrants oversight if the promised date is near. Note who called, what delivery information was provided, and that the case was added to the runner tracker for visibility.

The runner tracker is separated by department, including crown and bridge, removables, and full arch. An implant example belonged under crown and bridge. Capture the patient appointment and the call date; these help the representative set priority. A due date a week away was given as a lower-priority example, not as a universal rule.

## Late and at-risk cases

Source timestamp 00:34:03

For a late case, log the inquiry in ABS, communicate with the runner, and update the departmental tracker promptly. The session describes rapid response as an internal 30-minute response target. Involve a lead or manager when needed to keep visibility and follow-through. Do not turn that internal target into an unconditional customer promise.

For an at-risk case, compare production progress with the scheduled ship and delivery dates. A case due tomorrow without tracking needs urgent investigation even if the portal still displays its expected date. Include chair time, what remains in production, and the exact update needed in the tracker and escalation.

If the carrier tracking page gives an estimated delivery window, relay it explicitly as the carrier’s estimate. Do not personally guarantee that window. For a local driver and an appointment earlier than end of day, get a callback number and ask the shipping team to check whether the route can change.

The training discussed next-day air arriving around 10 or 10:30 a.m. in an example. If that would miss an earlier appointment, ask whether the office can move the patient later after the lab confirms the shipping plan. Carrier service and destination must be checked rather than treating 10:30 as a universal current guarantee.

Arrival on the promised calendar day can still be late for the patient. A case delivered at 10:30 for an 8 or 9 a.m. chair time merits complaint documentation even if the office is calm or agrees to wait. The complaint data should reflect the actual customer impact.

## Complaints and positive feedback

Source timestamp 00:40:20

When a case is late or communication fell short, acknowledge the office’s concern, gather accurate contact details, and explain that the team is investigating. Record a formal complaint in ABS rather than leaving the issue only in a general call note.

Use a clear subject such as late and lack of communication and select the appropriate complaint category. Assign it to the relevant business-unit leader so the production or service team can investigate root causes. The historical names were Melissa for full arch, Raj for crown and bridge and implants, and Javier for removables; logistics was temporarily under Raj. Check the current directory before using historical assignments.

For quality or other negative feedback, collect all useful case examples, file the complaint, and escalate to the lead group for departmental follow-up. Not every quality complaint needs a runner. Tell the office its feedback is appreciated and will be addressed with the team; avoid a dismissive statement that merely says a complaint will be logged.

Record positive feedback as well. Select positive feedback, choose the appropriate area such as customer service, on-time delivery, or quality, and assign the proper business unit. Write clean, professional notes because leaders and executives review that feedback.

## Technical incidents and scope limits

Source timestamp 00:57:50

If an office reports a technical incident or wants a clinician-level discussion, identify the case type and who planned it. The examples route a digital implant case to its digital planner and an analog case to its physical-case planner. A patient still in the chair can require immediate escalation or a runner to locate the technical person.

Use the supplied department and contact directory. If the correct owner is unclear, ask Cassandra or Sylvia and provide the case information so they can identify the right resource. Do not guess the technical answer or attempt to distinguish components beyond your knowledge.

For a potential case with no existing case or pan, use N/A for those identifiers in the handoff. Include the practice or doctor, requested topic, callback details, email, and availability. Answer only questions supported by the approved FAQ or your trained scope. Otherwise request technical assistance.

## Technical notes images and release from hold

Source timestamp 01:04:14

For a live case, check the prescription, shade information, and existing call notes. If a technical representative already asked questions, read those questions and obtain the office’s response. Record technical instructions under Technical Support rather than burying them in a general service note.

When images are received for a held case, upload them to ABS and email the appropriate department and the person who placed the hold. Use a handoff containing case number, pan, patient, and office or doctor. Attach the relevant images, describe the resolved requirement, and ask the department to review and proceed.

Include evidence of the outgoing communication in ABS, such as the sent-email text with its recipient and timestamp. This lets another CSR see what action was taken if the office calls again. The pan is especially useful on the production floor because it is visibly displayed on the physical tray; all identifiers on the pan, ticket, and case must agree.

Check Case Audit to verify when the hold was placed, its reason, when it was released, who changed it, and whether the case was rescheduled. The current case-information page may no longer display the old hold reason, while the audit preserves the history. Uploading the files or sending an email alone does not prove production resumed.

## Investigate discrepancies and close the communication loop

Source timestamp 01:13:03

If the office says information was sent but the case remains on hold, review the audit and communication trail internally. Do not assign blame or speculate to the customer. Explain that you are checking with production to provide accurate information, verify the best callback person and office hours, and follow up when more is known.

The trainer strongly discourages definitive callback durations because investigation may involve several people. Equally, do not prematurely tell an office that a case cannot ship or that it must reschedule the patient before the lab checks available options.

Compare scheduled and actual production dates. Missing scans or a case that has apparently stopped progressing should be escalated; a case unscanned for four days was an example of a red flag. Some departments were still improving scan compliance, so absence of a visible step required investigation rather than a firm conclusion.

The physical examples distinguish crowns, models, and dies. A missing model or die may be reproducible, particularly where digital records exist, but do not promise every item can be remade immediately. Ask the lab to evaluate what is available and needed.
