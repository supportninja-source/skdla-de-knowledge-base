## Investigate a wrong-office entry

Source timestamp 00:18:01

Compare ABS account fields with the original prescription in View Images. In the first scenario, a case entered for one doctor belongs to a different doctor with a similar surname. An incorrect account can prevent the intended office from seeing the case in its portal, send the physical item elsewhere, and bill the wrong office.

Verify the complete practice or doctor identity, patient, what was submitted or expected, submission timing, requested due date, chair time, caller name, callback number, email, and office hours. A surname alone is insufficient to distinguish the doctors in the example.

If records conflict, explain that investigation is needed and promise follow-up when more is known, rather than reading a misleading delivery status. Check whether the correct office already has a second case entered: the example has a duplicate fabrication while the incorrect office still has a charge.

## Recover the incorrect shipment

Source timestamp 00:23:07

On the outbound call, identify the lab, confirm whether the unexpected case arrived, and explain that a clerical error may have routed it incorrectly. Ask for help returning it, verify the label email, and obtain actual hours and lunch closures. Ask the office to confirm label receipt and track the return.

Recovery versus remake is decided with Cassandra, Sylvia, and the runner. The trainer contrasts an expensive item worth recovering with a low-value crown leadership might remake. Those illustrative prices are not agent approval thresholds. A manager’s invoice-zeroing workaround in this unusual example is not a standard edit for an agent to perform independently.

Document the voicemail, inbound calls, recovery request, label, complaint, and authorized billing action. Resolving the destination alone does not resolve duplicate fabrication or an incorrect charge. Contact the lead when the right correction is unclear.

## Schedule pickups around real availability

Source timestamp 00:30:01

The carrier examples use a three-hour pickup window. Ask the office for that day’s opening time, closing time, lunch closure, and time zone, then book a period when someone will be present. Verify the current carrier requirements during booking.

Do not rely only on internet hours. A practice may close early if it has no later appointments. A driver attempting pickup during lunch may not return until the following day. If the remaining window is too short, arrange the next feasible day instead of promising same-day service.

Time zones also affect callback urgency. The demonstration contrasts Pacific and Eastern time, but the practical requirement is to use the office’s actual local time and current availability.

## Distinguish entry mistakes from mixed models

Source timestamp 00:35:43

Ask what the office ordered, what it received, whether the patient is correct, and whether the model resembles that patient’s mouth. Cross-reference the prescription, restoration or tooth, work ticket, model markings, pan, and ABS case.

The trainer initially speaks of pan numbers engraved on models, then corrects the physical demonstration: the quadrant shown carries the case number, while some mini models show both pan and case identifiers. Read the actual markings rather than insisting every model uses the same scheme.

A matching case identifier with the wrong anatomy or tooth can indicate that a wrong digital impression was imported. A model identifier not matching the work ticket can indicate a mix-up in quality check or shipping. These are investigation clues to verify with the lab, not permission to assign blame before checking.

The demonstration distinguishes full-arch, quadrant, and mini models. Ask whether the crown seats on the model, what tooth was expected, and whether the anatomy matches the patient. This gives the lead evidence to choose recovery, remake, or technical review.

## Understand prescription images and scan access

Source timestamp 00:46:59

View Images contains the uploaded prescription and other associated documents or pictures; it is not the full STL scan viewer. Large digital scan files are stored on scanner platforms or the lab backend. Ordinary CSRs may need a lead with authorized portal access to inspect them.

Read the prescription’s doctor, patient, dates, tooth, material, shade, and instructions. Do not confuse an assistant or practice contact with the patient. Keep a tooth chart available to distinguish upper and lower, anterior and posterior, and quadrants. The chart and physical models are visual aids not supplied in the VTT.

## Follow the original and remake together

Source timestamp 00:52:52

In a swapped-case scenario, inspect both affected cases and search the patient name for later entries. An R pan prefix indicates repair or remake work, and a remake should reference its original case. An original record without that link or a meaningful note leaves the next CSR unable to understand the outcome.

The runner brings the prescription, work ticket, and pan to the lab’s data-entry department for an authorized remake. That department enters the replacement and supplies its new case number and estimated due date. Customer service coordinates, documents, escalates, and communicates; it does not treat this as new-customer onboarding.

Ask for the new case number as soon as it exists, record the new scheduled date and appointment, and put it on the tracker. The runner follows the replacement through production. Call the office with the new date and send tracking once dispatch is confirmed, so the remake does not repeat the original failure.

If two offices received the wrong cases, file complaints for both. Document what each office received and what each needs. Do not leave the history as an unnamed person will check and call us: get the person’s name and own the follow-up. Calling back for a missed fact is better than retaining an unusable note.

## Keep complaints concise but actionable

Source timestamp 01:08:10

The trainer replaces an old lengthy complaint template with short notes that retain the facts. Use a subject such as Complaint received wrong crown, select Complaint, choose the supported subtype, and assign the correct business-unit leader. Shortening prose does not remove classification or assignment requirements.

Identify the caller and practice, the problem and impact, appointment needs, action already taken, who is assisting, and the next follow-up. State concrete action such as working with the runner to re-enter the case and adding it to the tracker. Working on it by itself is too vague.

ABS already displays activity date and time, so avoid mechanically repeating them in a long narrative unless they distinguish a prior event. A final solution may still be pending, but the investigation and owner should be understandable.

Any negative experience merits a complaint, even when the caller is calm. Note frustration or repeated problems and escalate very upset customers to Cassandra or Sylvia. No complaint-reference number was available in this session; a leadership callback could be arranged.

Listen without interrupting, acknowledge the impact, appreciate the feedback, and explain the action. Complaint reports inform root-cause reviews, so accurate categories and customer impacts matter. Do not treat a low complaint tone as proof no complaint should be logged.

## Use careful delivery wording

Source timestamp 01:20:57

Say scheduled due date or the goal is for the case to arrive by the date, barring technical issues. Do not absolutely guarantee a delivery time. Carrier, weather, and production disruptions can change the outcome.

If the office has a required callback time, document and follow it. Do not casually offer a short deadline merely to finish a call. A promise to provide a new delivery date remains open until that date is communicated.

Leadership keeps the strategic-account manager or business-development contact informed of significant errors involving that account. The MB2 example preserves that email in ABS. Use the current directory to find the correct account owner.

## Invoice month affects the correction path

Source timestamp 01:23:26

The historical prior-month example cannot simply have its invoice removed and doctor renamed. The wrong office needs an authorized credit and the correct office’s case is entered appropriately. This is a manager or accounting action, not an agent entitlement.

For a current-month wrong-office shipment caught in transit, shipping arranges return to sender. An authorized manager removes the invoice, corrects the doctor, and arranges the correct shipment. Case Audit shows the invoice and account changes.

Check secondary tracking as well as the original return-to-sender record. A corrected invoice or a parcel back at the lab does not prove it was resent to the right office. Escalate missing secondary tracking, document the recovery, and file the complaint.

## Financial accommodations require approval

Source timestamp 01:27:57

A 50 percent goodwill adjustment is demonstrated for one delayed case, but agents have no fixed discretionary discount allowance in this session. Escalate requests to Cassandra for leadership evaluation; do not proactively lead a complaint with a discount offer.

Leadership considers case value, materials, repeated issues, margins, and already discounted bundles. The example says gold material cost cannot be discounted, and a zero-charge remake cannot be discounted further. Research the invoice before requesting a credit for work already free.

Acknowledge the request and say the team will evaluate and follow up. Do not guarantee approval or a percentage. The team must first establish how to fix the case or process, then decide the authorized financial accommodation.

## Return evaluation and a temporarily worn crown

Source timestamp 01:34:49

An office can return an unsatisfactory item for technical evaluation, but return does not guarantee credit. Ask what was wrong and request those correction instructions on the lab slip. Get the doctor’s preferred direct callback details if technical discussion is needed.

The temporary-crown example describes charging for a replacement while awaiting return and evaluation of the original, which may then be credited. The session names an Aspen-specific free-remake arrangement with an implant exception; this is historical account-specific guidance requiring current confirmation, not a universal offer.

Used returned work can be disinfected by receiving for evaluation. Warranty and credit decisions belong to the technical and authorized financial teams. A CSR cannot determine fault solely by phone.

Put return tracking in the notes so another CSR can check receipt. For recurring remakes, the trainer suggests the office call after the expected return transit, two days in that example, so the lab can prioritize it once received. Do not turn that example into a guaranteed evaluation turnaround.
