## Decide whether the office needs a label or a pickup

Source timestamp 00:06:31

Creating a shipment generates a label and can also schedule collection. Scheduling a pickup is a separate choice when the office already has its label. Always ask whether collection is actually needed: an office may have a regular driver or may drop the package at a carrier location.

The lab is charged for scheduled pickup attempts even if the office did not need collection. Do not schedule one merely because a label was requested. Confirm the requested service with the caller before completing the transaction.

## Set pickup dates and a usable collection window

Source timestamp 00:14:22

If collection is needed, select the UPS on-call pickup option and obtain operating hours, lunch closure, ready time, and location instructions. Check the chosen calendar date because the page can advance or skip dates while loading.

The training requires a three-hour pickup window for UPS and FedEx. Use the office's local time, not just Pacific time at the lab. An East Coast office at 3 p.m. with a 5 p.m. closure does not have the required three-hour same-day window. Discuss the next day or customer drop-off instead of promising an unavailable pickup.

Keep lunch closures outside the pickup window. Select office as the pickup location rather than front door so the driver enters the office instead of expecting a box outside. The trainer says a pickup does not require a recipient signature; she does not establish a definitive delivery-signature policy.

Normal deliveries are not scheduled for Saturday or Sunday in this training. Saturday can be considered as an exception through the appropriate team, especially when a lab-caused delay threatens a Monday appointment. Do not turn a demonstration example of Saturday timing into a carrier guarantee.

## Deliver the label and keep a shared record

Source timestamp 00:19:06

After completing the shipment, download the label as a PDF and email it to the verified office address with a short confirmation of the pickup arrangement. The carrier website can also email the label, but the trainer prefers sending the saved PDF through Outlook because some offices struggle with the formatting of the carrier-generated email.

Update the shared pickup spreadsheet with the doctor/account, email used, date, time, representative, request, and completion information. The same shared record covers UPS, FedEx, and local-driver requests. It allows a second representative to identify the original worker and verify where the label was sent if the office calls again.

## Coordinate local pickups and supplies separately

Source timestamp 00:22:39

Local-driver pickups go in the dedicated Teams pickup conversation with requested date, doctor or office, complete operating hours, and special notes. Early requests, often before 10 a.m. in this session, may fit that day's route, but route progress and staffing determine the result. For uncertain same-day requests, record the next available day's hours and warn the office that service may move.

Return to the conversation to confirm acknowledgment. The logistics team or Sylvia may give a thumbs-up, check mark, or written response naming the driver. If no confirmation appears, follow up rather than assuming a posted request is scheduled. The account of a driver being sick demonstrates that even an early request can need rescheduling.

Local pickups do not need a carrier label. Supply orders such as boxes, preprinted labels, prescription forms, and biohazard bags go in the designated pickup/supply spreadsheet area, described as orange highlighting with bold black text. Repeated unfulfilled supply requests should be escalated directly to the client leads; the pickup conversation is specifically for pickups and should not be cluttered with supply-order discussion.

Use the holiday-hours sheet when relevant to record office closures so shipping can hold or time deliveries appropriately. Shipping staff move fulfilled requests into the fulfillment file; customer service should not rearrange those records merely to mark its own work complete.

## Understand the three-system workflow

Source timestamp 00:39:21

The doctor portal allows offices to submit files, ask questions, view cases and tracking, create labels, and make bill payments. Portal activity creates a HubSpot help-desk ticket. ABS is the production case record. At the time of training, the systems were only partly integrated: a file submission did not enter or attach itself to ABS simply because a HubSpot ticket existed.

The customer-service team used HubSpot principally for these portal tickets, not for its normal inbound or outbound phone calls. An administrator reviewed and delegated tickets because automated categorization was still being refined. Incorrect automatic assignment should be reviewed rather than assumed to be authoritative.

The trainer also offers portal-access assistance after resolving the caller's immediate request. The message is to make the portal useful, not to refuse help until the caller uses it.

## Process a genuine new upload

Source timestamp 00:46:52

Open the assigned ticket and identify the exact account or clinic, patient, and upload. In the portal, choose the matching clinic and go to Files. Similar clinic names and multiple portal accounts are possible, so match the submitting account rather than opening the first similar name.

Read the uploaded description and prescription information, then download the associated file. In the historical handoff template, include office/account details, patient, phone or other required account information, and the Rx description. Case and PAN can be marked not applicable when no live case exists.

Send the information and correct downloaded attachment to the appropriate Teams data-entry conversation. Customer service consolidates and hands off the submission; the data-entry team creates the case in ABS. This was described as an interim arrangement pending more direct routing.

Check the template every time. Replace the old patient, account, description, and attachment when reusing a prior handoff. A saved template that still names a previous office can cause entry under the wrong account. Match the downloaded ZIP/file identifier when multiple results exist; the trainer uses its last four characters as an additional cross-check.

If essential information such as tooth number or shade is absent, ask the office before claiming entry is complete. After a completed handoff with no further action outstanding, send the acknowledgment, assign the ticket to yourself for accurate productivity attribution, and close it. An acknowledgment that the team is working on entry is different from confirming the case has already been entered.

## Do not treat every upload as a new case

Source timestamp 00:49:08

An upload may be photographs for an existing production case, a technical update, a new case, or images sent ahead of a physical case. Check the context before choosing a workflow. If no matching case is found, ask whether the office is sending it digitally or as a physical submission.

When images belong to an upcoming or returning case that has not arrived, keep the ticket in Processing or the appropriate hold state. Add an internal note explaining that entry/arrival is pending and the images will be attached to the correct new case. Do not attach them to an old case just to clear the ticket.

The assigned representative remains responsible for checking ABS daily, updating the pending-work note, and following up with the office when the expected case remains absent. Once the correct case is entered, attach the images and complete the ticket. The example remains open for weeks because the physical impression had not yet returned; an old similar denture case was not the correct destination.

## Technical updates and ticket ownership

Source timestamp 00:53:19

The trainer believes the desired response target may be 20 minutes and says tickets were set high to encourage prompt handling, but she explicitly does not confirm the exact reassignment timer or whether it varies by pipeline. Treat these as unresolved settings, not a contractual SLA.

Technical tickets follow the corresponding call workflow: acknowledge the information, send it to the responsible production department, and include the person who placed the hold when the update addresses that hold. Routine tier-one tickets were intended to be within the team's skill scope, with more technical cases reviewed or escalated.

Close a ticket only when the relevant action has been completed or the remaining operational follow-up is properly owned in the applicable workflow. Keep an image-awaiting-arrival ticket open. Record why any ticket is pending so an administrator does not mistake it for neglected work.

## Investigate shipping tickets and missing tracking

Source timestamp 01:22:02

Use the ticket case number to open the correct ABS record. Read the matching Call notes entry first to understand the actual shipping question; a Shipping label in HubSpot is only a category. Then review status, production steps, invoice timing, and tracking.

An invoice posted after the shipping cutoff may explain why there is no tracking number yet. In the example, the case was invoiced late in the evening after that day's shipping had finished. The trainers settle on telling the office that tracking should appear in the portal by end of day on the shipping day, rather than promising a specific clock time.

The discussion describes approximately 4:30 p.m. as the end of ordinary shipping work in that example and notes exceptions. Confirm the current cutoff. A completed/invoiced case expected to ship on the next business day did not need the runner tracker merely to generate routine tracking. If offering to email tracking later, own that follow-up. For a rush or earlier-than-normal requested date, use the separate feasibility workflow.

## Respond on the appropriate platform and avoid duplicate sends

Source timestamp 01:26:57

For an existing active case, the demonstrated approach is to respond through the portal as a Support note so the message is visible to the office and reflected in ABS, and copy the response into HubSpot. A HubSpot-only reply did not reliably appear in ABS at that time. Internal and Support notes have different audiences; check the actual type before sending rather than relying solely on color or an assumed default.

Use a clear opening, direct answer, closing, correct grammar, and your name. A writing assistant can polish the wording, but the representative supplies and verifies the facts. Click Send; changing ticket status to Closed does not automatically send a HubSpot draft. Save ABS notes before changing cases because unsaved text can disappear, unlike a retained HubSpot draft.

The portal can look as though it is still loading after a send. Repeated clicking may send duplicates. Check the actual posted message before retrying. The trainers were expecting integration changes but had not confirmed them, so verify the current synchronization behavior before continuing a historical double-response process.

Duplicate tickets may be documented and consolidated, but the trainer notes that tickets closed without a recognized customer response sometimes reopen automatically. Do not assume a merge feature or automatic closure behavior that was not demonstrated.

## Escalate earlier requested delivery dates

Source timestamp 01:39:38

Check when the case arrived, the current expected delivery, notes, fabrication, and production stage. A request for an earlier date than normal turnaround is a rush/expedite assessment, not a reason to overwrite the date or guarantee shipment.

Use the correct department runner tracker, such as crown and bridge for the demonstrated digital implant case. Search first to avoid a duplicate entry when several tickets already cover the same inquiry. Enter case number, PAN, representative, relevant account and requested action; leave the runner's response fields for the runner.

Mark the request as a portal inquiry, include the requested date and any appointment information already in the case notes, and distinguish unknown appointment information from a fact. Follow the runner's response and tell the office a more accurate date when confirmed. The trainer uses moderate priority for the demonstrated request; urgency must depend on the actual situation, not on the mere presence of a date request.
