## Research a remake and warranty request

Source timestamp 00:27:20

Open the ABS case from the ticket and cross-reference the doctor and practice/billing information. Go to Call notes and read the actual concern. In the example, the office requests a rushed, no-charge remake of a fractured Maryland bridge using the old design.

The trainer states that the original appliance must return to the lab for evaluation before the team can determine whether the remake qualifies under warranty. Customer service cannot simply authorize no charge because the office requests it. Starting a remake before the original returns may incur an additional charge pending evaluation. Explain the policy and ask how the office wants to proceed rather than promising free work.

Review previous cases, remake history, fabrication, original submission method, and the returned-components information. R on the demonstrated PAN signified a remake. Ask for the original restoration and relevant models, impressions, or other components that production needs so the case is not delayed again by missing materials. The exact set depends on the case; do not turn one example's component count into a universal requirement.

## Confirm that a digital design is still available

Source timestamp 00:33:41

A request to reuse an old digital design requires verification that the scan or design is still available. The trainer searches the original case to identify its scan platform, then checks the relevant platform through authorized access. In the example, the original platform was iTero and the scan could not be located there.

Failure to see a scan in that platform does not by itself establish that the lab no longer has the design. Ask the runner to check with the digital team. If neither usable digital information nor the necessary physical materials exist, production may need a new scan. Do not promise a remake from the old design while availability remains unconfirmed.

Use a structured internal inquiry containing the case and PAN, patient, doctor/account information, what the office wants, and exactly what needs confirmation. Include the fact that the office is returning the appliance and has asked for a rush. These notes anonymize the live example rather than repeating patient identifiers.

## Keep unresolved remake work in Processing

Source timestamp 00:48:19

Acknowledge the office's request while stating what remains unconfirmed. The example response says the team is verifying whether the original design is available and asks whether the original appliance has been shipped back.

Respond on the portal for the associated case so the communication is reflected in ABS, and also make the HubSpot status and note clear. Set the main ticket to Processing while awaiting the runner or design answer. Do not mark the overall inquiry finished merely because an acknowledgment was sent.

The trainer refers to templates and uses a writing assistant to polish wording, but a reusable signature feature was not established and the available template slots were reportedly full. Verify facts, identify yourself in the response, and do not assume an AI-generated reply has validated the case.

When several tickets cover the identical case and question, keep one working ticket open. Close a duplicate only after noting that the main ticket is open and the runner is working on it, with ownership clear. This reduces duplicate open work without losing the unresolved action.

## Read the actual shipping inquiry before checking a link

Source timestamp 00:58:33

Copy the associated case number into ABS after clearing old search fields. Remove accidental leading or trailing spaces if the case does not appear. Confirm the correct office and case, then read the matching Call notes entry rather than going straight to a generic shipping screen.

The example complaint is that the portal shows an invalid tracking number and the office needs an ETA. Research shows that a FedEx number was attached to a link opening the UPS website. Review the carrier in Case info and track through the correct carrier. Do not declare the number invalid merely because the wrong carrier cannot find it.

Once verified, provide the actual carrier's current status and expected window and include the correct tracking link. Explain the clerical carrier-link mismatch without making up a new tracking number or saying the package is lost. The numerical-length comparisons in the discussion are informal recognition clues, not a definitive carrier-validation algorithm.

## Distinguish invoiced from collected and delivered

Source timestamp 01:44:09

For a shipping inquiry, read the shipping-related Call note and compare the inquiry date with actual completion, invoice, and tracking events. An older ticket may already be resolved by delivery, so report current verified status rather than repeating what was true when the request first arrived.

Invoiced indicates that production completion/invoicing has occurred in this explanation, but does not prove that a tracking label has been generated or a carrier has collected the package. The sequence can be invoice, set aside for shipping, label creation, then collection.

A tracking number generated earlier in the afternoon may still show the package at the lab. The trainer describes carrier pickup around 5 p.m. Pacific in this historical workflow, so tracking movement may not populate until after collection. Explain the distinction between label created and package actually left the lab.

If an invoiced case lacks tracking and you have promised an update, ask the runner or coordinate through the lead with logistics to obtain the number. For a local Southern California office, the case may instead be hand-delivered by the local driver and have no UPS/FedEx number. Confirm local eligibility and route; do not assume all California offices use local delivery.

For an unusual local request, ask the coordinating team what can be done and call the office back when confirmed. Do not commit to a special driver visit before feasibility is assessed.
