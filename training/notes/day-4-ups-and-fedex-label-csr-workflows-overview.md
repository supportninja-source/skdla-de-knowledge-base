## Separate the driver from the runner

Source timestamp 00:25:42

The local driver transports cases between the lab and offices. The runner works inside the lab, locating cases and monitoring production. Their operational records are different: driver pickup/delivery requests go through the logistics conversation and route list; case-status inquiries go to the runner's tracker.

The carrier three-hour pickup window does not apply to the local drivers. Local-driver feasibility depends on geography, traffic, route progress, staffing, and the day's dispatch obligations. Ask Sylvia for an ETA when a case is already on a driver's route because she has direct contact with the drivers. Do not promise a time from the route list alone.

## Research a case-status answer

Source timestamp 00:57:02

Find the correct case, review Case info, original and current due dates, prescription, notes, and Steps and service schedules. Compare where the case is against where it should be by the current date. An internal remake can change the outlook even when the displayed due date looks reassuring.

Use qualified language such as the current delivery goal barring technical issues only when the evidence supports it. If uncertain, tell the office you will investigate and call back with accurate information. When the lab needs more time, proactive outreach is the lab's responsibility; an unexplained changed due date is a reason to research the communication history.

Log the contact in ABS even when a case is already completed and does not need a production tracker entry. Another representative must be able to see who gave the information, what date was discussed, and what was promised.

## Locate difficult and combined cases

## Evaluate and document rush inquiries

Source timestamp 01:55:45

The preferred approach is to receive the case in the lab before confirming whether the rush is feasible and what fee applies. For pre-arrival inquiries, obtain doctor/account status, whether this is an existing customer, fabrication, digital or physical format, required turnaround or due date, and a good callback number. If the office insists on an assessment before sending it, send the details to the runner for production review. Relay only the confirmed feasibility, fee, and condition that the case must arrive by a specific time or date. Recap the arrangement in email and retain follow-up reminders, particularly when no live ABS case exists yet. Check whether the case actually arrived and follow up if the expected arrival is missed.
